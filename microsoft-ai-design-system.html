Motion

MAI motion is editorial and intentional — slow, deliberate reveals that feel human. Interactions are fast and responsive. Marquee and scroll-linked animations are signature patterns.

Hover any row to preview: the dot travels the track with that curve (pausing briefly at each end), and the plotted bézier shows its shape.

Editorial reveal--ease-editorial · cubic-bezier(0.25, 0.1, 0, 1)

Motion Principles

Editorial Reveals
Hero headlines animate on load with a slow upward fade (opacity 0→1, translateY 20px→0) over 1000ms with ease-editorial. Italic words may animate slightly after the roman weight.
Scroll-Linked
Section headlines and image-text pairs stagger in as they enter viewport. Each staggered child delays by 80ms. Use IntersectionObserver with threshold 0.15.
Continuous Ticker
The marquee pattern repeats brand phrases at 18s/cycle. Always pause on hover (animation-play-state: paused) and respect prefers-reduced-motion.
Card Lift
Cards translate -4px Y with box-shadow elevation on hover. Duration 250ms ease-out. Never scale cards — translate only.
Ghost Button Arrow
The trailing → on ghost CTAs translates +4px X on hover over 150ms. Communicates directionality with restraint.
Reduced Motion
All animations and transitions collapse to 0.01ms when prefers-reduced-motion: reduce is set. Marquee stops completely.
Foundation · 06

Iconography

MAI uses minimal inline SVG icons and abstract SVG illustrations. Icons are single-weight line style at 20px/24px. Illustrations are soft, generative abstract patterns — not flat icon sets.

Open source

Source: Phosphor Icons

Phosphor (MIT licensed) is the closest open-source match to MAI's existing in-house glyphs — thin single-weight strokes with rounded caps and joins at 1.5px. Where a system's house style fits another free library better (e.g. Lucide, Tabler, Material Symbols), use that; if the best fit is paid or unavailable, degrade to Material Symbols as the open-source default. Brand glyphs below are custom in-house SVGs — request their source files from the design system admin.

Foundation · 07

Shadows & Highlights

Elevation expressed through soft, low-contrast shadows. MAI keeps elevation restrained — shadows imply layering, never drama.

Elevation / Shadows

--shadow-sm · subtle lift (cards, inputs)
--shadow-md · raised (hover, buttons)
--shadow-lg · floating (popovers, lifted cards)
--shadow-xl · overlay (modals, dialogs)

Highlights / Glows

Highlights are the inverse of shadows: in light mode, depth reads as a soft cast shadow below an element; in dark mode, cast shadows nearly disappear, so elevation is carried by a faint top highlight where light catches the upper edge. Toggle dark mode (top right) to see the effect. Glows are reserved for focus and accent emphasis.

--highlight-top · top-edge elevation (reads in dark mode)
--highlight-focus · focus ring / selected state
--highlight-accent · gold glow for emphasis moments
Foundation · 08

Photography & Imagery

MAI uses imagery that feels human first — warm, candid, and unguarded. Photography of people anchors the brand in real life. Abstract SVG and generative art handle technical and model-related concepts.

Imagery Types

People Photography
Candid moments of real people in natural environments — conversations, gestures, outdoor settings. Warm ambient light. Never stock-photo stiff. Used in hero, team, and section splits.
Abstract / Generative SVG
Pale, low-contrast SVG patterns — sound waves, diagonal lines, soft radial shapes. Used exclusively as model card backgrounds and decorative overlays. Never busy or high-contrast.
Photo Collage
Grid of 6–9 square photos on a beige background connected by thin lines. Used for news thumbnails about multi-subject topics. Feels editorial and curated, not random.
Painterly Illustration
Colorful, warm AI-generated cityscapes and park scenes. Used exclusively for office location cards. Loose, impressionist style — vibrant but never garish. Consistent warm palette.
Technical / Data Art
Abstract waveform and graph-like imagery for audio/transcription content. Dark background with gold or white line art. Used for MAI-Transcribe-1 and MAI-Voice-1 news thumbnails.
Product screenshot overlay
Product Screenshot
Product UI screenshots overlaid on blurred nature photography. Used for Copilot, Bing, Edge product cards. Scenic background softened to 40% opacity blur; UI sharp on top.

Color Treatment

People & Lifestyle Photos
  • → Warm color temperature (5500–6500K). No cool blue tones.
  • → Natural, slightly lifted shadows — never crushed blacks in skin tones.
  • → Gentle highlight roll-off. Avoid blown-out backgrounds.
  • → Saturation: moderate. Colors are present but not punchy.
  • → No heavy filters, presets, or stylized color grades.
Illustrations & Abstract Art
  • → Painterly illustrations: high saturation, vibrant primaries, impressionist looseness.
  • → Abstract SVGs: pale cream/white on near-white. Near-invisible, texture only.
  • → Beige (#F5F0E8) is the universal background for collage imagery.
  • → Dark technical art: ink-black background, gold or white line art only.

People Photography Principles

✓ Do
  • ✓ Candid, in-the-moment shots — people mid-gesture, mid-laugh, mid-thought.
  • ✓ Diverse representation across age, ethnicity, gender, and ability.
  • ✓ Natural environments — offices, outdoors, urban settings with authentic context.
  • ✓ Warm natural or diffused light — sunlight through windows, outdoor ambient.
  • ✓ Shallow depth of field with soft, blurred backgrounds (f/1.8–2.8).
  • ✓ Eye contact with the camera or natural off-camera glances — both work.
  • ✓ Emotional warmth — joy, curiosity, focus, connection.
✗ Don't
  • ✗ Stiff, posed corporate headshots against plain white backgrounds.
  • ✗ Overly retouched skin — maintain natural texture and authenticity.
  • ✗ Cool blue or desaturated color grades — off-brand and cold in tone.
  • ✗ Clichéd tech tropes — pointing at whiteboards, handshakes, laptop-in-café.
  • ✗ High-contrast dramatic lighting — this brand is warm, not cinematic.
  • ✗ Stock photos with forced smiles or overly staged "diversity" setups.
  • ✗ Overlaid text directly on faces or obscuring the subject.

When to Use Photography vs. Illustration

Context Type Rationale
Hero / homepage sections Real photography Humanizes the brand; people = mission made visible
Team profiles ("Life at MAI") Real photography Authenticity is critical; no illustration substitutes
News / blog thumbnails Photography or collage Subject-dependent; abstract for technical topics
Office / location cards Painterly illustration Consistent, ownable visual style across 8 global cities
Model cards (MAI-Voice, MAI-Image) Abstract SVG Texture without distraction; models are the hero
Audio / transcription content Technical data art Waveform and graph shapes signal technical depth
Product showcase (Copilot, Bing) Screenshot + nature photo Grounds UI in the real world; nature = warmth + aspiration
